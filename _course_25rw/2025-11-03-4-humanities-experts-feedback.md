### 3. Anthropologist (Ethnographic Methods) ⭐⭐⭐

**KEY INSIGHTS:**
- Football case study makes abstract concepts accessible
- Bromberger's characteristics provide useful analytical framework

**CRITICAL CONCERNS:**
- **Lack of ethnographic depth:** Prioritizes theory over lived experience; participant observation would reveal emotional weight (e.g., football chant vs. prayer)
- **Prescription vs. practice gap:** Assumes rituals function as intended; ethnography shows people subvert, adapt, or ignore prescribed forms
- **Missing voices:** How do informants describe their own rituals? Their language often differs from scholarly taxonomies

**RECOMMENDATIONS:**
- Add ethnographic vignettes (e.g., fan's account of match-day ritual) to ground theory in practice
- Discuss materiality: Highlight objects (jerseys, rosaries) and bodily actions (kneeling, chanting) for embodiment

### 5. Philologist (Text-Critical Methods) ⭐⭐

**KEY INSIGHTS:**
- Modern theoretical frameworks overlook philological roots: Latin *ritus*, Greek *telein*, Arabic *ṣalāt*
- Bromberger's "anti-structure" echoes Turner's *communitas*, but philological analysis of inversion motifs (Saturnalia, *walima*) would ground this in textual traditions

**CRITICAL CONCERNS:**
- No engagement with primary textual witnesses (e.g., *Mishnah*'s *Seder Mo'ed*, *Rigveda*'s *yajña* hymns); variants reveal debates over ritual efficacy
- Conflates modern analogies with ancient categories without addressing how terms like *leitourgia* or *'ibādah* were historically contested

**RECOMMENDATIONS:**
- Introduce 10-minute segment on etymological case studies (e.g., *ritus* in *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum* vs. *sunna* in early Islamic papyri)
- Assign critical comparison of ritual manual (*Shulchan Aruch*, *Yajurveda Samhita*) with Bromberger's characteristics using variant readings

### 6. Literature Post-doc (Author/Reader Perspectives) ⭐⭐⭐½

**KEY INSIGHTS:**
- Employs **analogical rhetoric** using football as defamiliarization strategy (Shklovsky) to prompt critical reflection
- Turner and Grimes adopt **poetic condensation** ("powerful moment," "embodied enactment"), inviting students to engage as *readers* interpreting layered meanings

**CRITICAL CONCERNS:**
- **Substantialist/functionalist binary** risks oversimplifying ritual's polysemy; religious students may resist functionalist reductions
- Bromberger's characteristics could **flatten cultural specificity**; students might misread rituals as universally "anti-structural"

**RECOMMENDATIONS:**
- **Add reception-focused activity:** Have students analyze ritual through *both* emic (participant) and etic (observer) lenses
- **Incorporate counter-narratives:** Include excerpt from practitioner's account (e.g., footballer's memoir) to disrupt authoritative voice
